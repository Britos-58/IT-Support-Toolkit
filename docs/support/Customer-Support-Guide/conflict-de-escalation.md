# Conflict & De-escalation

In IT Support, you will encounter angry, stressed, or rude users. The goal is to de-escalate the emotion so you can focus on solving the technical problem.

## 1. The H.E.A.T. Framework
When dealing with an upset user, follow the H.E.A.T. method:

- **H - Hear them out:** Listen without interrupting. Let them vent. Often, users just want to feel heard.
- **E - Empathize:** Acknowledge their feelings. *"I completely understand why you are upset. Not having access to your email during a product launch is incredibly stressful."*
- **A - Apologize:** Apologize for the situation, even if it is not your personal fault. *"I am sorry that you are dealing with this disruption today."*
- **T - Take Action:** Pivot immediately to the solution. *"Here is what we are going to do right now to get this fixed..."*

## 2. What NOT to Do
- **Never say "Calm down":** This has the opposite effect and invalidates their frustration.
- **Never blame the user:** Even if the user clearly broke the rules (e.g., clicked a phishing link, spilled coffee on the laptop), do not lecture them during the crisis. Fix the issue first.
- **Never blame other IT teams:** Do not say, *"The Network team messed up the firewall."* Present a unified front. Say, *"We are experiencing a network routing issue, and our engineers are working on it."*

## 3. When to Escalate to Management
You are not required to endure abusive behavior. Escalate to the IT Manager or Service Desk Lead if:
- The user uses profanity directed at you (e.g., name-calling).
- The user makes threats.
- The user refuses to follow standard security protocols (e.g., *"I don't care about the policy, give me Domain Admin rights right now!"*).
- **Action:** Politely state, *"I understand this is critical, but I do not have the authorization to bypass that security policy. Let me get my manager on the line to assist you further."*
