# Communication Guidelines & Etiquette

Professional communication builds trust between the IT department and the rest of the business. Follow these guidelines for all verbal and written interactions.

## 1. Ticket Etiquette (Written Communication)
Every update in an ITSM tool (like Jira or ServiceNow) is a permanent record. 
- **Public Notes (Visible to User):** Must be professional, clear, and free of technical jargon.
  - *Bad:* "Flushed DNS and rebuilt the OST file."
  - *Good:* "I have cleared the network cache and synchronized your mailbox. Your emails should now be updating correctly."
- **Internal Notes (Visible only to IT):** Must be highly technical and detailed. If you escalate the ticket, the next engineer needs to know exactly what commands you ran.
  - *Example:* "Ran Test-NetConnection on port 443, TCP failed. Checked AD account, no lockouts. Escalating to Network team to check firewall logs."

## 2. Active Listening (Verbal Communication)
When a user calls the Helpdesk, they are often stressed.
- **Do not interrupt:** Let the user finish explaining the issue, even if you already know the solution in the first 5 seconds. Interrupting makes them feel dismissed.
- **Acknowledge and Validate:** Use phrases like *"I understand how frustrating it is to lose your work"* or *"I see why this is blocking your presentation."*
- **The "Echo" Technique:** Repeat the core problem back to them to confirm understanding. *"Just to make sure I have this right, the printer on the 3rd floor is printing blank pages, correct?"*

## 3. Managing Expectations
Never promise a resolution time unless you are 100% certain.
- **Under-promise, Over-deliver:** If you think a fix will take 30 minutes, tell the user it will take an hour. If you finish in 30 minutes, you look like a hero. If you hit a roadblock, you still have a buffer.
- **The "Next Steps" Rule:** Never end a call or a ticket note without telling the user what happens next. 
  - *Example:* "I am going to consult with the database team. I will call you back before 3:00 PM with an update." (And make sure you actually call them back, even if the update is just "We are still working on it").
